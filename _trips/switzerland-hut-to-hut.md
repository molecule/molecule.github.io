Over the course of 4 days, we hiked from Kandersteg, to Blüemlisalphütte, to Gspaltenhornhütte, to Rotstockhütte, to Mürren. Here are all the details about our trip!

#### Prep
First, we hired Alex from [WanderWeGo](https://www.wanderwego.com/home/hut-hiking-in-switzerland) to help plan the trip. We went with the consultation package, which was incredibly helpful - she eyeballed our plans, made recommendations, and taught me how to use the four crucial apps. The trip would not have gone as well as it did without her - I will definitely be trying to find a local expert to help all of our future trip planning.

There are four apps you need to use to plan and carry out a trip to Switzerland:
- SwissTopo
- SwissMobility
- SBB - for train trip planning
- MeteoSwiss - for weather

#### Day 1 - Kandersteg to Blüemlisalphütte
<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="30%" alt="Day 1 stats" src="/src/img/schweiz/day1-stats.png">

We spent the night in Kandersteg, at a beautiful hotel with a sauna and a pool, which was an excellent pre-trip relaxation experience. Then, on the morning of the firs day, we took the Rodelbahn gondola up from Kandersteg, and walked along a very chill walkway to Oeschinensee lake.


<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Oeschinensee lake" src="/src/img/schweiz/oeschinensee-view.jpg">

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Oeschinensee lake" src="/src/img/schweiz/oeschinensee-view2.jpg">

The lake is absolutely stunningly, unreal-level gorgeous. The main regret I have about our trip was that we didn't budget in more time to spend at the lake. I would have LOVED to boat around, swim, and just enjoy the crazy views more than we did.

After the lake, it starts to go uphill and it basically doesn't stop. In total, this hike was estimated to take 4 hrs.  For us, from top of Gondola (11am) to taking off shoes in the hut (6pm), it took 7 hours. We of course stopped to take pictures, and to rest, and to eat lunch, but, still. It is NOT an easy hike (and this is the easy route to Blüemlisalphütte).

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 1 - glacier view" src="/src/img/schweiz/day1-2.jpg">

This is the best view of the glacier, which was so so cool to see, and also the first view of the hut. With no exaggeration, I felt pretty extreme despair at this point, because I was soooo tired (we were 5 hrs in to the hike at this point), and we still has soooo far to go (it took us another 2 hours from here).

That pic on the right was the last one I took before we got to the hut - we were really struggling at this point. It's about 2000 meters above sea-level, or close to 9000 feet, which is super dang high, and we'd been hiking for 5 hours at that point. We'd walk 10 steps, then stop for a 2-minute break, and then walk 10 more steps.

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 1 - inside dorm" src="/src/img/schweiz/day1-4.jpg">

We did eventually make it, and I basically just laid down in the hut. Dinner was a super light (and delicious) soup, cheesy noodles with ham chunks, and lemon cake. To be completely brutally honest, I didn't love the view from the hut. You're kind of ON the mountain range, but I prefer to look AT the mountain range.

All in all, I wouldn't recommend this hike. It was exhausting, the hut was crowded, and the view at the end was not great. So, hike elsewhere, and then come back and visit Oeschinensee lake. The traditional route is to actually start in Griesalp and hike UP to Blüemlisalphütte, and then down to Kandersteg (so, sort of the reverse of what we did). It definitely sounds nice to descend to Oeschinensee, but otherwise, I'd just skip this hut.

#### Day 2 - Blüemlisalphütte to Gspaltenhornhütte

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="30%" alt="Day 2 stats" src="/src/img/schweiz/day2-stats.png">

Woke up bright-eyed and bushy-tailed, and at breakfast in the hut. Breakfast is advertised as "hearty" but it's oatmeal, toast, and tea. They do offer something called "marching tea" or "hiker punch" - which is some kind of warm drink. It's not....*good*, but, definitely get it.

From the Hohturli pass, it's the steepest stairs you've ever imagined. It's SO steep - I imagine going up feels more like climbing a ladder. And off to your right is crumbly scree - if you slipped you'd be in trouble. Both Matt and I have a good head for heights, and dang - this is bananas:

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 2 stairs" src="/src/img/schweiz/day2-1.jpg">

Going down is a billion times easier than up, and even though this part was pretty steep, it was stunningly beautiful every step, so we enjoyed every moment. 

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 2 view" src="/src/img/schweiz/day2-2.jpg">

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 2 first view of hut" src="/src/img/schweiz/day2-3.jpg">

We didn't realize it at the time, but that pic was actually our first view of the hut. It's juuuuuust above the tip of Matt's hat. We rested for about an hour by this beautiful waterfall.

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/3h8U7cykFaM?si=nPOumP2puziPEvLI" style="padding-inline: 25px; border: none; width: 50%; height: 400px;"></iframe>

Dinner was great, a super light and delicous soup, then pasta with bolognese sauce, and a super funky pineapple mousse dessert. It started raining before the sun went down, and didn't stop until 2am.

This was my favourite hut. It felt extremely modern and beautiful - basically brand new from top to bottom.

This hike is supposed to take 3.5 hrs, and from Hohturli pass (9am) to arriving at the hut (4pm), this hike took us 6 hours. We took about an hour break for lunch, and another hour break by the waterfall. The last 100m to the hut is basically straight up loose scree and then some steps - not super fun.

I definitely recommend this hut, though. The view was excellent, and the next leg was my favourite hike I've ever done.


#### Day 3 - Gspaltenhornhütte to Rotstockhütte
<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="30%" alt="Day 3 stats" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-stats.png">

By far my favourite day. Unbelievably gorgeous hike, and super fun, too, with a ladder!! Once you walk down from the hut, you're not walking in the valley, you're walking along the mountainside, hundreds of meters above the valley floor. There are cables along the narrowest sections, so it feels pretty comfortable. I mean, LOOK at this view. The fog was rolling in and out every 10-15 minutes, like the entire landscape was breathing.

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 3" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-1.jpg">

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 3" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-2.jpg">

We booked this trip a littttle bit at the last minute, and did not look in detail at every part of the trail, so we were pretty surprised to hear that this section had a ladder. Anyway, it was actually super chill, and honestly super fun to climb a ladder with a backpacking backpack on.

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 3" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-3.jpg">

This is coming up to the top of Sefinenfurgge pass. Can you see the little nob on the mountain behind Matt? Yeah - that's Blüemlisalphütte. Where we stayed the first night.

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 3" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-4.jpg">

<iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/VeHNmtjrc2A?si=WKRQzRJ3bWFF0VW0" style="padding-inline: 25px; border: none; width: 50%; height: 400px;"></iframe>

Unfortunately, I don't really have any pictures from the pass itself - it was insanely windy, and very very very steep and narrow, and I really didn't feel comfortable taking my phone out. But, it was also super beautiful. 

This is the last part of the pass, just before descending to Rotstockhütte.

<img style="border: 1px solid #000" width="100%" alt="Day 3" src="/src/img/schweiz/day3-5.jpg">

We left Gspaltenhorn hut at 8:30am, and reached Sefinenfurgge pass at 11:30am, and then took a very leisurely meander down and made it to Rotstockhütte by 2:30pm, for a total of 6 hours.
